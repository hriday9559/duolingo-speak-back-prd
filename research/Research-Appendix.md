# Speak Back — Research Appendix

Companion to the Speak Back PRD. This repository copy documents the research protocol, findings and reporting limitations for the independent portfolio project.

## Research status

### Survey
The survey combines two Google Forms collected over approximately six months. There were 55 merged responses and 53 retained after cleaning. Respondents came primarily from the author's network; results are directional and not representative of Duolingo's user base.

### Interviews
Six real interviews were conducted in November 2026: three active and three lapsed Duolingo learners. Each interview lasted approximately 15 minutes. The source material consists of interview notes, not verbatim transcripts.

Themes recorded in the notes include difficulty turning learned material into natural speech, preference for asynchronous voice messages over live calls, and differing preferences for practising with friends versus matched learners. One participant specifically valued being able to re-record. Theme counts are qualitative observations from six interviews, not population estimates. Refer to `Research-Appendix-v1-full-text.md` for the detailed interview synthesis.

### Prototype usability test
Three participants tested the six-screen interactive Speak Back prototype. All three found the core experience understandable, and no major usability problems were reported. Some participants raised safety concerns; these were discussed in relation to the safeguards proposed in the PRD. Some participants also suggested optional AI analysis of voice messages, with the user choosing which individual message may be analysed.

The prototype simulated recording and playback and did not capture actual microphone audio. This test therefore evaluated comprehension and navigation of the proposed flow, not actual voice-message behaviour, audio quality, willingness to exchange messages, retention impact or product-market fit. The sample is very small and qualitative.

## Reporting rules

- Report survey counts as “X of N”; include the denominator.
- Treat the small, network-based survey sample as directional, not proof.
- Report interview and usability findings as qualitative observations, not population estimates.
- Separate observed findings from assumptions, hypotheses and proposed targets.
- Do not describe interview notes as verbatim transcripts.
- Do not imply that simulated prototype testing validates real audio functionality or retention impact.

## Interview prompts

1. Tell me about the last time you used Duolingo.
2. What usually makes you open it?
3. Have you tried speaking the language outside the app? Tell me about the most recent time.
4. What happened when you tried to speak?
5. What do you currently do when you want speaking practice?
6. Have you ever practised with a friend or another learner? What was that experience like?
7. After exploring the Speak Back concept, what would make you try it or avoid it?

## Prototype feedback and product implication

The initial prototype flow was understandable to the three participants who tested it. No major interaction redesign was indicated by this small test. Safety concerns reinforce the importance of visible and effective reporting, blocking, access controls and moderation.

The suggestion for AI analysis of selected voice messages is recorded as a post-MVP opportunity, not a validated MVP requirement. The proposed principle is explicit, message-level user consent, with feedback private by default. Further validation is needed for demand, feedback quality, privacy, technical feasibility and cost.

## Survey source and cleaning

The survey workbook records 55 merged responses and 53 cleaned responses. Cleaning included removing two duplicate submissions, normalizing text formatting and blanking invalid shifted answers rather than guessing. Identical responses on different days and some inconsistent conditional answers were flagged but retained. Refer to the cleaned analysis for exact handling and denominators.
